# viz-os: design notes

For people working on the plugin. The skill files never point here: rules an agent needs live in `skills/deck/`.

## Decisions (and why)
| Decision | Why | Where |
|---|---|---|
| The agent writes only `deck.yaml`; one kit draws everything | Style drift in long outputs comes from hand-written HTML/CSS. If design is not in the model's output, it cannot drift. It also saves tokens: the 30-slide MAGeCK deck is about 3,900 words of YAML, against tens of thousands of tokens for hand-written HTML. | `kit/`, `SKILL.md` |
| Rules that long outputs forget are lint checks, not prose | Prose rules decay with output length; a linter does not. The checks: every displayed symbol has a meaning, the ladder order, derived columns have formulas, every working step has its what and why, maths is not typed into prose, text budgets, checkpoint spacing, abbreviations, retired keys. | `scripts/build.py` |
| One expression language for derive, tables, calc, ops, equations, chart functions and `{{text}}` | The formula that computes a number is the formula shown to the learner, so the two cannot disagree; numbers are never typed. | `kit/core.js` |
| Aggregates show their recipe (`mean(x)` → (1/n)Σxᵢ → (8+34+…)/6) | The failure in earlier decks was "x̄ = x̄ = 22.5": a number with no visible logic. | `L.tex` in `core.js` |
| Numbers in worked lines carry their names underneath, once per slide | Anonymous numbers ("0.05152 − 0.009313") were the core complaint about earlier calc pages. Labelling once per slide keeps the clutter down. | `opLabels` in `slides.js` |
| Probe before building: goal, pretest, what they know, which comparison clicks (length only when unsaid) | Prior knowledge decides the entry rung (expertise reversal); a pretest helps memory of the questioned material; anchors make the bridges personal. 3–4 questions keep the reply to one short line. | `probe.md` |
| A `lead` line on each slide ("But…", "So…") | It makes the causal chain visible and catches "and then" gaps. The lint warns when fewer than half the slides have one. | `story.md` §4 |
| One look, flat and square, after proff-viz 5.0.0 | The user asked for the older proff-viz look as the template ("old plugin as template"). Near-black page `#0b0c0e`; content on plates `#171819` with thick grey borders `#565653` and hard offset shadows; heavy Inter headlines, JetBrains Mono tags; one orange `#ff5a36` for the answer and what matters now, never a background. No blur and no backdrop filters, so other browsers draw what Chromium checked. The `theme` key is retired (the lint warns), so every deck looks the same. History: moonlit, then an orange-glow wallpaper, then proff mono grey, then iOS-style glass (1.0.0), then this. | `vizos.css` |
| Series colours blue `#7fb0ff`, mint `#5fdcc4`, magenta `#c274aa`; grey `#b6b4ad` for context | Measured on 2026-10-24 with the CIEDE2000 colour difference (ΔE00; the code was checked against Sharma, Wu & Dalal 2005, pair 1 = 2.0425). Colour blindness was simulated with the Machado, Oliveira & Fernandes (2009) matrices at full severity, in linear RGB. Every pair among the three series and the orange: ≥ 31.5 for normal vision, ≥ 15.9 for protan and deutan (worst: mint vs magenta, deutan), ≥ 10.7 for tritan (blue vs mint). The 2.0 draft's third colour, violet `#c89cf2`, was 2.4 (protan) and 3.8 (deutan) from the blue: nearly the same colour for a red-green colour-blind reader. Magenta was the best third colour in the violet–magenta band of a search over OKLCH (lightness 0.60–0.92, chroma 0.09–0.18, contrast ≥ 4.5:1 on the raised plate `#242526`). Lime and yellow scored a little higher (19.2, 19.1) but were not taken: lime is close in hue to the green "good" colour, and a bright yellow competes with the orange for attention. The weakest pair left is mint vs the context grey (7.7 protan, 6.8 deutan); grey reference lines are also dashed, so they do not rely on colour alone. WCAG contrast on the plate: blue 8.1, mint 10.6, magenta 5.4, orange 5.7, grey 8.6 (all ≥ 4.5:1, so each can carry text). | `vizos.css`, series order in `chart.js`, term colours in `slides.js` |
| Two stages: 1600×900 landscape and 720×1280 portrait; only the landscape one is checked | The portrait stage exists because one scaled 16:9 stage makes text about 7px on a phone. At 2.0.0 the user said phone checks were not needed, so `check.py` opens a 1440 × 900 desktop screen only, and the lint no longer limits columns for phone width (the desktop audit warns when a matrix is crowded). The portrait stage stays in the kit, unchecked. | `deck.js`, `vizos.css`, `scripts/check.py` |
| Content that runs long is zoomed down (never below 70%), and the audit warns below 80% | Robust to slightly-too-long slides without hiding the problem. The fit measures each card's content against the card's own padding, because a grid or flex child can stick out of its card without changing the card's `scrollHeight`; the audit flags any text that spills out of its card. The fit runs again after an answer opens (only ever shrinking, so nothing jumps), and a fit started for a slide already left is dropped. | `fitSlide`, `L.refit`, `L.audit` in `deck.js` |
| The browser check also re-fits every slide with animations on | During 2.0 work the fit was measured while slides were still animating in, so every phone slide shrank to 70% for real viewers. The check, with animations off, saw full size and passed. Now it re-fits each slide live and warns when a viewer would see it shrunk differently. It also opens every predict answer and audits the slide again ("after the answer"). | `scripts/check.py` |
| Contrast is measured on pixels, against a screenshot with the text hidden | Labels over chart marks and over tinted cells are where legibility fails. Per-line fragments avoid false readings from chips beside the text. | `scripts/check.py` |
| YAML 1.2 booleans (`No` stays text) and one-line YAML errors with a fix hint | Both were hit while writing the example deck: `[Yes, about 2×, No, …]` split on commas, `No` became `false`, and a `?` broke a flow map. | `build.py`, `spec.md` |
| Compact headlines (46px), wider visuals (text column 27%) | The user asked for smaller headings and bigger plots. | `vizos.css` |
| Visual first: the linter counts the words on each slide | The user's feedback: slides were text-heavy and the story should come through the visuals. proff-viz's own floor says the same ("the figure is the page": short labels on the figure, ≤ 40 words of labels per step). Limits: about 40 words on an idea slide, ≤ 2 short points beside a visual, titles ≤ 8 words, captions ≤ 20; an idea slide without a visual is warned. Labels inside a chart, flow or parts bar have their own limits. | `build.py` |
| The formula is the figure: each term is named under it | Names sit next to the thing they name, and the same colour links a term to its number in the worked line. Saying it aloud and the code moved behind "+ more", one tap away. | `slides.js` (`termLabels`) |
| `parts` visual: a whole split into named pieces, or one bar per row | Variance = counting part + wobble part was told in tables and algebra; the sizes were never seen. One bar shows a split; rows show how the split changes (the wobble's share as the mean grows). | `slides.js` |
| No formula from nowhere: `from:` and `kin:` on every equation | The user's feedback: formulas appeared out of nowhere. They wanted an idea of how each one is reached (not the full derivation) and how it relates to formulas in other topics. Each equation shows a short chain from a form the learner knows, one move per link (CPM = a percentage counted per million; the Poisson = the binomial with many rare tries), and 1–3 relatives with the same shape elsewhere (run rate, parts per million). On a wide screen the chain is a column beside the formula that flows down and points across into it; on a phone it is a band above. The linter warns when an equation has no `from`. | `slides.js` (`T.equation`), `build.py`, `story.md` §7 |
| Tables one step at a time | The user wanted step-by-step calculation in tables. Step 1 names the given columns and what each holds; then a column (or a row, or a single number) per step. The cells a formula reads are underlined in the colour their symbol wears in the formula, so each number can be followed from the table into the working (signalling plus spatial contiguity). All steps' working shares one grid cell, so the card never changes size between steps. Any computed number can be tapped for its own working. | `slides.js` (`T.table`) |
| Term labels: above the formula for numerators and powers, below for the rest; slide before dropping a row | Labels under a stacked fraction hung on one vertical leader and the lower leader cut through the upper label. Now the side is set by the term's height against the `=` sign, a label slides sideways while its leader still lands inside it, neighbours are pushed left as a group to open a lane, and a label drops a row only when that fails. Wrapped labels shrink to their longest line. The audit flags any label that still has to cross. | `termLabels` in `slides.js` |
| `ops` slides for matrix steps: the row or column being computed is lit, the shape change is named, one row is worked in numbers | The user could not see whether a step ran along rows, down columns or over the whole matrix, or how the data changed shape (counts → ratios → one number per guide). Each step is one operation (`rows`, `cols`, `map`, `dot`, `sort`, `vec`) with its shape before and after. The matrix shows the mechanics only; a chart after it carries the result. | `kit/ops.js`, `spec.md` (Matrix steps) |
| `dist` visual for named distributions | The user wanted distributions shown with their shape and parameters. A `dist` names the family, says what each parameter means, gives the mean and variance with their formulas, and can shade a tail. A slider moves the parameter that is the lesson. | `slides.js`, `chart.js`, `spec.md` (Distributions) |
| Every step says what and why | The old decks showed tables "not really specifying why". `why:` on working slides, `say:` and `why:` on `calc` lines and `ops` steps, and `says:` on computed columns are short and linted, so the reason sits next to the step it explains. | `build.py`, `slides.js`, `ops.js` |
| A worked line must add up by hand | A line's `fmt` used to round the numbers going in as well as the result: 2.648 showed as 3, and "3 × 418^1.22 = 4,291" was off by about 10%. Now `fmt` rounds only the result. The numbers going in get up to 2 extra digits until the shown result follows from them; otherwise the line reads ≈ and the check warns. | `subFmt` in `slides.js`, `L.audit` in `deck.js` |
| Real-method facts come from the code, with the version; numbers come from a seeded script and an independent recompute | Papers and code disagree. MAGeCK 0.5.9.5 computes each guide's p-value from a normal tail (its negative binomial p-value function is commented out), while the paper describes negative binomial p-values. The deck teaches the code and says so. `make_data.py` writes `data.json`; `verify_data.py` recomputes every number with separate code; `check.py` writes `numbers.txt` with every number the slides show. An agent that did not write the deck can compare all three. | `story.md` §11, `examples/mageck-rra/`, `check.py` |
| Chapter shapes and notation chips | The user wanted to see how the data changes type and shape, and to learn the notation. A chapter names the shape it produces, and its divider shows "in → out". The first slide that uses a symbol (Σ, a hat, `x_ij`, Φ) gets a small chip that says what it means. | `slides.js`, `spec.md` |
| Pictures only on request, with no text in them | An image model costs time and tokens and cannot spell labels reliably. `scripts/image.py` runs only when the user asks; the kit draws the labels on top of the picture. | `scripts/image.py`, `spec.md` (Visuals) |
| The class skill's board is a deck slide type added through an `ext` hook, not a fork of the deck | One kit, one look. `build.py` takes an optional `ext` (lint fields, extra JS and CSS); with no `ext` the deck output is byte-identical, checked with `cmp` on all three examples. | `deck/scripts/build.py`, `class/scripts/lesson.py` |
| The board draws its own slider instead of reusing the deck's `controls()`, and on a lesson page it wears the class look | Exporting `controls()` from `slides.js` would change the deck's built bytes. The board's slider registers in `ctx.ctl` and calls `ctx.refresh()`, so the check can drive it like any deck slider. On the page each slider, run and toggle takes one line the figure's width, name, line, value, with no boxes: the user picked this on the options sheet, and it keeps a row from ever being wider than its figure. | `class/kit/board.js`, `class/kit/board.css` |
| Worked lines on the board have their own renderer: symbol = formula, = numbers, = result, with term colours as text colour | The deck's `calc` box could not light the table row a line reads. The board writes each input's symbol, its number and the table cell it came from in one term colour (a symbol's `color:` wins, else table columns take s1, s2, s3), on the current line only; past lines go grey. The user asked for coloured text over underline bars ("text coloring … with underlines being less common") after finding bars plus tints too much. | `class/kit/board.js` (`tint`, `light`), `board.css` |
| A long scene just runs on down the page, with no fit and no hidden scroll | Stage 1 of the scrolling page. The old rule grew the slide and let the deck's fit shrink it; on a page nothing shrinks, so type and figures keep their sizes. A scene is still capped at 10 steps, because it is hard to hold in mind, not because it must fit. | `class/kit/board.css`, `lesson_lint.py` |
| Focus by colour (orange now, grey before), never by fading | Faded text failed the contrast check and read as "not part of the lesson". Past steps stay readable in the grey text colour. | `board.css` |
| Highlights go on the thing itself (row, column, cell, item), never as marks drawn over the board | Drawn circles and boxes were placed from screen positions and the user said they would often land in the wrong place. `mark:` now lights a table row (the deck's row look), writes a column or cell in orange, or rings a figure or card with the orange shadow (a block with no box takes a bar), for one click, and only on things already shown. `==s1:text==` in a teacher line is bold text in a term colour; plain `==text==` keeps the deck's orange underline, so underlines stay rare. | `board.js` (`light`, `md`), `board.css`, `lesson.py` (`MARK`) |
| A think stop takes two clicks: the question, then the answer | The learner should guess before seeing the answer (the pretesting evidence below). The teacher band shows "Pause · think" on the first click and "So" on the second. | `board.js`, `board.css` |
| An assembled formula shows our numbers first, then the same formula in symbols on the next click, in one strip under the work | Seeing your own numbers in the formula's shape before the symbols makes each symbol a name for a number you already worked (CampusX style). Each `plug` number is tagged `[name:number]` so it takes its term's colour, and the lint warns on an untagged number. The strip sits under the work it sums up, or on top when the scene opens with it, so lines added later read below it. | `board.js` (`drawAsm`, `strip-top`), `board.css` (`.has-strip`), `lesson.py` (`lint_formula`) |
| Board items reuse the deck's own renderers (chart, image, stat, ops, compare), never a second copy | One look and one audit for both skills. A visual's `step` counts board clicks; an `ops` item's matrix steps take the next clicks, so the lint asks for one teacher line per matrix step. | `board.js` (`visual`, `opsl`), `lesson.py` |
| A board number that data.json already holds must come out the same (KTD21) | The page and `make_data.py` are two computations of one number; a silent mismatch teaches a wrong value. The audit warns with both numbers. | `board.js` (`store`), `tools/class-cases/calc-vs-data.yaml` |
| A quiz with 7 or 8 cards goes four across with smaller type (question 26px, answer 24px) instead of shrinking the slide | Eight cards in three columns shrank to 70%, under the 80% floor. Four across with the answer face sized to fit keeps the cap at 8 at full size (measured: zoom 1.0, every answer face fits). | `deck/kit/slides.js` (`T.quiz`), `vizos.css` (`.quiz.dense`) |
| Plan KTD7 ("ink": circles and arrows drawn over the board) is dropped; `mark:` highlights do its job | The user turned down drawn marks in U2 (see the highlights row above). A highlight on a target that is not there is reported by the audit ("mark target not found"), which was the point of the ink test. | `tools/class-cases/mark-missing-target.yaml` |
| A run (`run: 12`) and a toggle (`toggle: [off, on]`) are written as a plain slider by `lesson.py` before the lint and the page see them | The browser check already moves every slider to both ends, so a run is audited at its first and last frame and a toggle in both states with no change to `check.py`. | `class/scripts/lesson.py` (`prepare`), `board.js` |
| Each click puts a run on its frame (`frame:` on a step, else the `stops` in click order); a redraw leaves the frame alone | A redraw happens whenever a slider moves or the fit runs. If it reset the frame, dragging the scrubber would snap back. The board marks a redraw in `layout` and only a plain `show` (a click) sets the frame. | `board.js` (`redraw`, `frameAt`) |
| Play is off until pressed, stops at the last frame or on any click, and does nothing under `?check` | A moving picture the learner did not start pulls attention from the teacher line; the check needs a still page to measure. | `board.js` |
| A control that a think question asks you to use appears with the question, not the answer | `step_at` on a control maps to the first click of its step. The toggle in the gallery's run scene was hidden on the very click that asked for it. | `board.js` (`firstOf`) |
| Controls sit under the text column when there is one, beside the numbers they move | Under the plane they took its height: one scale on both axes makes a plane as small as its shorter side. | `board.js` |
| Plane names keep clear of dots, rings, drawn lines and square edges, with twelve places to try; a free `text` label is centred on its point first | A name on a line or under a ring was hard to read in the gallery. When none of the twelve places is clear, the audit warns instead of drawing on top. | `board.js` (`name`, `hit`), `tools/class-cases/plane-overlap.yaml` |
| A run's data is chosen by a seeded search in `make_data.py`, with the teaching claims asserted | The gallery's perceptron needs exactly 11 updates and cells 0.8 apart so a ring points at one cell. The think answer says each update fixes its own cell, so `make_data.py` asserts it and `verify_data.py` checks it again with separate code. | `class/examples/board-gallery/make_data.py` |
| The lesson lint lives in its own file, `class/scripts/lesson_lint.py`, run through the deck build's lint hook | `lesson.py` stays the build glue (kit files, `prepare`); the rules have one home. `prepare` keeps a copy of `data.json` for the lint, so the deck's `build.py` did not change for it. | `tools/check_class.py` |
| Lesson-shape rules (plan first, problem before formula, limits before next, next last, 8–14 scenes) are warnings, and a test case that is only a fragment expects `builds`, not `clean` | An off switch in the lesson file would be the first thing an agent reaches for to silence a warning. A fragment case states "0 errors and a clean browser check", plus `not:` lines for the messages it must not raise. | `tools/class-cases/minimal-board.yaml` |
| A symbol may show on the same click as its number (a worked line, a parameter card); an assembled formula's symbols row counts on the click after its numbers row | A worked line writes `d₁ = x₁ − x̄ = 4.3 − 5.70` in one go: the numbers are right there. The formula strip is built to show numbers first. | `tools/class-cases/symbol-before-number.yaml` |
| A name inside `{{…}}` counts as its number showing; a symbol's TeX inside `$…$` counts as its symbol showing, longest TeX first | Text matching is the cheapest test that catches the real failure (a formula written in symbols before any number). `s^2` is matched before `s`, so a variance does not count as the SD. A TeX spelled two ways (`d_1`, `d_{1}`) is missed; see the backlog. | `tools/class-cases/symbol-before-number.yaml` |
| The typed-number lint reads only keys a learner reads (`say`, `q`, `a`, `note`, `label`, `caption`, …) | Expressions, coordinates and plane marks hold numbers on purpose. A list of prose keys stays right as kit fields are added; a list of keys to skip would not. | `tools/class-cases/typed-number-ok.yaml` |
| The board gallery is a full lesson (plan, problem, limits, next, cards last) | It is the regression test for the lesson-shape rules as well as the scene kinds, so it must pass them with 0 warnings. | `examples/board-gallery/lesson.yaml` |
| `recall:` copies the earlier lessons' review cards into this lesson at build time, and prefixes each data name in their `{{}}` with `r<k>_` (k = its place in `recall:`) | The kit's number language runs only in the page, so the build cannot pre-format numbers; prefixing lets two lessons share a name (`xbar`) and each card still shows the number its own lesson showed (AE1). The values are copied from the earlier `data.json`, so the page stays self-contained. `include:` was rejected: it appends slides at the end. | `class/scripts/lesson.py`, `tools/class-cases/recall-ae1.yaml` |
| The recall quiz order: the last lesson listed under `recall:` is the latest; it gives its first cards, and each older lesson adds its first card, up to 5 | KTD17. Recall the most recent lesson most, and keep one thread to each older one. | `tools/class-cases/recall-order.yaml` |
| The symbol ledger and the typed-number lint skip the recall quiz | Its cards were linted in their own lesson, and their symbols were taught there; checking them again would flag `$\bar{x}$` before this lesson's own number. | `class/scripts/lesson_lint.py` (`make_lint`) |
| The build never writes to the viz-os home; `memory.py` does, when the agent calls it | A build must be repeatable and free of side effects; memory is the Remember step's job. Every `memory.py` write is idempotent, and its `learner` verb edits only `history:` and `home:` as text, so the deck's fields and the learner's comments survive. | `tools/check_memory.py` |
| Home data is pure Python (`random.Random`), one key and one table row per line, with its template, seed, sizes and recipe version recorded | The same seed gives the same bytes on any machine without numpy, `home.py --check` proves a file is what its seed makes, and a diff reads row by row. Cells × markers keeps a row per cell, genes × samples a row per gene (R21). | `class/scripts/home.py`, `examples/home/cells.json` |
| A lesson's `home: <name>` names its home table, and `make_data.py` copies the rows it uses into `data.json` with `"home": {name, provenance}` from the home file | Each lesson stays self-contained: the build reads only `data.json`, so a lesson rebuilds after the home folder is gone, and the provenance says which template and seed made its rows. A nested `home` block in `data.json` builds and checks clean (tested on a copy of the gallery). | `class/board.md`, Series |
| The perceptron lesson starts from the line (9, −2.1, 0.9) with η = 0.1, and its second line in limits starts from zeros | A start that calls every B cell T makes the trick needed at once, and gives a run of 12 updates over 4 epochs with a plus case (c7) to stop on. Seeded starts near the first line all ended within 3° of it, so a second start from the same region could not show that the trick stops at *a* line, not the best one; zeros end at a clearly different line (gap 0.175 vs 2.121 from the nearest cell). Changing either changes every number the think answers quote. | `class/examples/perceptron-trick/make_data.py` (`START`, `ZERO`) |
| A lesson is a deck file with board scenes in it: the title and recap are deck slides | The deck's deck-level rules (question, recap near the end, learner block, bridge rules) keep running with no copy. `board` is not a deck content type, so the deck's lead and checkpoint counts skip it; the lesson lint counts leads and think stops instead (plan KTD14). | `class/scripts/lesson.py`, `lesson_lint.py` |
| A lesson is one long page, and a figure sits beside its lines | The user asked for the CampusX board: a page that fills up, where written things stay and the teacher scrolls back. Steps that appear by click hid earlier work and forced every scene into a fixed stage. So the page reads by scrolling, and only a think answer waits. A think answer takes no room until pressed: the card grows from a fixed top, and what the step adds waits in its own space. | `class/kit/lesson.js`, `board.js`, `board.css` |
| Every figure owns its shape: a plane's from its axis ranges, a chart's from `ratio`, an image's from the image | The user: figures and tables never take an odd shape to fit a layout. The layout gives a width and a height limit, never a box to fill, and an audit fails a chart outside 1–2.2 or a plane outside 0.6–1.8. | `class/kit/board.js` |
| A lesson has no quiz and no recall cards; think stops carry the questions | The user removed them: they want the one-line questions inside the lesson, not cards at the ends. A later lesson continues through memory, its `lead` lines, the last `next` scene and a think stop near the start, computed from its own data. `lint_cards`, `lint_recall` and `lesson.recall` stay in class because sketch keeps its review cards. | `class/scripts/lesson_lint.py` |
| A finished lesson is tidied to `lesson.html` and `lesson.yaml`, and the working files go to an attic, not the bin | The user wants a folder with one or two important files. A move can be undone with `--restore`, so nothing is lost, and a later lesson can get a `data.json` back. It refuses unless the page stands alone, including a page built from older data, and it refuses inside `skills/`, because the examples are regression tests. | `class/scripts/tidy.py` |
| The Think and So tag is 15 px | The page's smallest text allowed is 15 px (`MIN_TEXT` in `lesson.js`), so the audit would fail anything smaller. The look sheet's smaller tag could not stay. | `class/kit/board.css` |
| A figure with text under it goes up to 1280 | The user set this on the options sheet (stage 1c). `parts` and `dist` had their own 1060 px limit, and now take the scene's wide-figure width, so they match its other figures. | `class/kit/board.js` |
| The step bars keep 10 px of invisible padding above and below their 3 px line | The user chose thin 3 px bars, but a 3 px target is hard to hit with a pointer. Padding gives a 23 px target and no visible change. | `class/kit/board.css` |
| Next stays under the pointer: a press moves it less than 2 px | A learner keeps pressing without moving the mouse. A sticky figure past its top is held up by the foot of its block, so a think answer that grew the text column dropped the figure, 79 px in `pca-2-how`, and the next click missed. Keeping the room open from the start would break "answers take no room until pressed", and scrolling would move the text being read. So the figure holds its place, and lets go as the reader scrolls back. | `hold` in `class/kit/board.js` |
| The line by the buttons steps aside while the same words are on screen, and keeps its room | The line exists so that Next never leaves the learner's words out of sight, so it cannot go just because those words are somewhere in the scene: they always are. The test is "on screen now". The learner sees the words once, never twice and never not at all, and the page does not jump. | `S.quiet` in `class/kit/board.js` |
| Geometry goes in a board `plane`, one scale on both axes, unless it says `fill: true`; the deck chart stays for charts | A projection's right angle, a square on a distance and an arrow's turn only look right at equal scale, which the deck chart cannot give. Histograms, curves and run traces stay deck charts (plan KTD8). Equal units tie a plane's width to its height, so a plane can sit small; `fill` gives it the chart shape with a scale per axis. Equal units stay the default because the failures differ: a plain scatter left small is harmless, but a stretched geometry picture is wrong and looks right. So `fill` with a `vector`, `foot` or `square` is a lint error. | `class/kit/board.js` (`plane`), `class/scripts/lesson_lint.py` |
| A lesson's figure is sized first from 70% of the page; the side column then takes all that is left, never under 30% (and never under 300 px) | This reverses the 70 / 30 rule chosen at stage 1b. A short window stops a figure short, and the fixed split then left a blank strip round the figure while the text sat in a narrow column. The user's marked-up pictures asked for the text to take that room. The figure sits at the left of the page column, 20 px from the text, so the text's left edge moves with the figure's width; that is the price of no blank strip. ## Evidence behind the story rules
Sources were checked by web search during 1.0.0 work; effect sizes are as reported by those sources.
- **Pretesting / prequestions.** St. Hilaire, Chan & Ahn (2024), *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, preregistered meta-analysis: specific effect g = 0.54 (k = 97), general effect g ≈ 0.04 (k = 91). Guessing helps memory of the questioned material, not the rest. Narrative review: Pan & Carpenter (2023), *Educational Psychology Review* 35:97. Hence the pretest is about the core mechanism, and the deck reveals it.
- **Comparison and analogy.** Alfieri, Nokes-Malach & Schunn (2013), *Educational Psychologist* 48(2): case comparison beats single-case study (reported d ≈ 0.50; one summary says 0.60). Bridging analogies and "anchoring intuitions": Clement (1993), *Journal of Research in Science Teaching* 30(10). Hence `bridge` slides with explicit pairs and `breaks`.
- **Concrete to abstract.** Fyfe, McNeil, Son & Goldstone (2014), *Educational Psychology Review* 26: start concrete, fade to abstract. A 2020 review questions how far this carries into advanced science (*Educational Psychology Review*, 10.1007/s10648-020-09581-7). Hence numbers before symbols, without dropping the general form.
- **Prior knowledge.** Expertise reversal effect: Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller (2003), *Educational Psychologist* 38. Hence the probe sets the entry rung and skips known rungs.
- **Names first.** Mayer's pre-training principle: learning improves when the names and characteristics of key concepts come first. Hence the `name` move and `symbols` with meanings.
- **Signalling.** Schneider, Beege, Nebel & Rey (2018), *Educational Research Review* 23 (103 studies): signalling helps retention and transfer, more for low prior knowledge. Hence colour-linked terms, the orange accent and the "Look:" captions.
- **Narrative.** Dahlstrom (2014), *PNAS* 111 (Suppl. 4): narrative raises comprehension and engagement for non-experts. Hence the spine and the but/so chain.
- **Comparing cases, and explaining why.** Comparing two analogous cases helps learners pull out the shared structure and transfer it: Gentner, Loewenstein & Thompson (2003), *Journal of Educational Psychology* 95, 393–408 (comparing two cases beat studying them one at a time). Explaining why a fact holds (elaborative interrogation) and self-explanation were rated moderate utility in Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1), doi 10.1177/1529100612453266, with larger benefits when the learner has more prior knowledge. Hence `from:` (why this form, from one the learner knows) and `kin:` (the same shape in other topics). Checked by web search on 2026-10-24.
- **Fewer words, labels on the picture.** Coherence principle (leave out extraneous words and pictures): supported in 23 of 23 experimental tests, median effect size 0.86, per Mayer & Fiorella's chapter in the *Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning* (2nd ed., 2014; an earlier count was 10 of 11 tests, median 1.32). Spatial contiguity (put words next to the part of the picture they describe): Ginns (2006), *Learning and Instruction* 16, 511–525, a meta-analysis of spatial and temporal contiguity. Hence the word limits, captions that say where to look, and term names under the formula. These are lab effects on short lessons; none tested viz-os.
Uncertainty: these are lab and classroom effects on average; none tested viz-os itself. The honest test is whether decks built with the probe and bridges beat decks without them for this learner, with the same topics.
